How it's calculated

  • Each row contributes current × fraction of time. Time rows: duration × count ÷ period. Minutes-per-day rows: minutes ÷ 1440. A "rest of period" row takes whatever is left; several of them split it evenly.
  • mW rows are converted to battery current as P ÷ (Vavg × efficiency). Enter µA/mA/A when you measured current at the cell itself.
  • Self-discharge is added as a constant drain of capacity × rate ÷ 730 h. Typical LiPo is 2–5 %/month; it only matters for sleepy µA-class designs.
  • Usable fraction covers the cutoff voltage, ageing and temperature. 80–90 % is a reasonable planning figure for a 3.0–3.3 V cutoff on a healthy cell.
  • Event budget: charge per event = Σ current × duration; events = usable capacity ÷ charge per event. It assumes nothing else draws from the cell (no sleep current, no self-discharge), so it's the upper bound. To see an event alongside a real sleep profile, add it to the load profile at a daily rate.
  • Runtime = capacity × usable ÷ average current. It ignores peak-current sag, so very high burst currents on a small cell will do somewhat worse.
